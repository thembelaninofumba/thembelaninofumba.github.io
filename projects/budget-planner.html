The idea

A budget should make the trade-offs easier to see. I built this planner around a simple question: once expenses and savings are accounted for, how much of this month’s income is still available?

How it works

  • Choose a month and enter take-home pay in South African rand.
  • Edit category names and amounts, add rows, or remove items that do not apply.
  • See the totals and chart update as the budget changes.
  • Get clear feedback when planned spending exceeds income.
  • Download a CSV containing income, budget items, and totals.

Small details that matter

The calculation layer uses decimal arithmetic and rounds to cents. Savings are part of the budget total, so the amount left reflects the full plan.

The documented example starts with R15,000 income and R11,000 budgeted, leaving R4,000. It gives someone an immediate starting point they can replace with their own figures.

Scope & limitations

This is a planning tool for expected amounts. It does not connect to a bank or track actual transactions. Edits last for the current session; downloading the CSV is the way to keep a copy. Selecting a different month does not load a saved budget.

The source is public on GitHub, with tests for calculations, rounding, invalid inputs, overspending, CSV export, and interface changes.
